Reading the Cell Ontology

CL is a graph, not a list. This is the structure the review queue is built on, worked through with real terms and real flags — every example below comes from the 2026-06-08 release and from the audit's own results.

← back to the proposal queue

1 The basic edge is is_a

A term points at the thing it is a kind of. Follow the arrows and you reach progressively coarser claims about the same cell. Nothing else is asserted: the edge says a neutrophil is a kind of granulocyte, not what a neutrophil looks like.

Why it matters here. A reasoner can walk these edges perfectly and still tell you nothing about whether a cluster is a neutrophil. What would help is what CL has least of: marker-based sufficient conditions, present for about 9% of classes. One of the proposals in the queue is exactly that, for neutrophil.

2 A term can have several parents

The graph is a DAG, not a tree. Cell identity is genuinely not a partition, and CL records that rather than forcing a choice.

3 Anchor sets

Rather than compare terms directly, the audit walks a term's ancestors and records which of eight coarse roots it reaches. That set is its anchor set — a summary of what kind of thing it is, robust to how finely it happens to be named.

4 Disagreement, made visible

A cluster's label and its expression are two independent claims. Resolve each to a CL term, take both anchor sets, and ask whether they intersect. When they do not, the claims disagree about lineage — a strong signal, because crossing one of eight roots takes more than a naming quibble.

5 What this cannot see

The property that makes anchor sets precise also makes them blind. If a label is wrong but wrong within one lineage, both terms reach the same anchor, the sets intersect, and nothing fires.

This is the common case. 21 of 28 curated errors stay inside a single lineage — which is why the audit ships a second tier, a ranked marker queue asking which term best explains the cluster, rather than the anchor sweep alone.

6 When the graph is missing an edge

Sometimes the disagreement is not about the data. Two terms can describe overlapping populations with no is_a path between them in either direction, so a cluster assigned to one registers as disagreeing with the other — and no expression evidence can settle it, because the question is structural.

Whether a subclass relation belongs there is a curatorial judgement, not something an audit can decide. What the audit contributes is that the relation is absent and the scale at which it bites — which is the shape of the missing-axiom proposal in the queue.
